# Evidence-based style evaluation

Apply only when the author explicitly requests analysis, review, or training.
Read the relevant [shared cards](style-contract.md), the literal passage, and
the complete relevant source evidence. Do not add a reviewer to normal writing.
This protocol organizes judgments; reliable agreement with author ranking must
still be demonstrated independently.

## Fix the reading surface

Record whether the target is an opening, selected excerpt, or full scene, its
literal boundaries, version, and the task/context available to the reader.
Judge that surface only. Later events cannot repair an opening's missing
experience. Mark omitted context or incomplete source reads as limitations.
Read the selected series' lexical dictionary when present. Check unwanted words in their actual sense and word forms; judge preferred words by fitness and repetition, not presence counts. The two lists are independent preferences, not replacement mappings. Report an evidenced lexical miss separately from canon, causality and the holistic style judgment; keep source quotes, canonical terms and explicit task exceptions intact. An empty list or another series' list supplies no rule for this passage.
For blind comparisons, withhold identities, historical rankings, and author
ratings until judgments are frozen; use the same context for each candidate.

## Annotate before comparing

Separate three judgments:

- **Correctness:** literal events, knowledge, referents, staging, and causality.
  Distinguish demonstrated defects from ambiguous interpretations.
- **Mechanism:** what is actually observable under an applicable card.
- **Reader effect:** holistic voice, lexical naturalness, rhythm, development,
  and strength of impression. A reconstructable mechanism does not prove this
  effect. Explain the reading with literal evidence and mark inference.

Use direct passage annotations or the following table. Keep quotations short
but sufficient to locate the transition; include source coordinates.

| Passage/quote | Card ID and condition | Evidence and alternative reading | Classification | Effect/uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `<literal tested span>` | `<ID; why applicable>` | `<literal source comparison; proxy or exception checked>` | `<one classification>` | `<supported effect or unknown>` |

Allowed mechanism classifications:

- `evidenced-consistent`: evidence supports the applicable mechanism; no claim
  of overall style success follows automatically.
- `evidenced-deviation`: a literal departure is supported after checking the
  rule's condition, exceptions, and plausible competing readings.
- `not-applicable`: the stated applicability condition is absent.
- `insufficient-evidence`: source, context, or interpretation cannot support
  the requested judgment; identify the missing evidence.

Do not count classifications as points, prescribe numeric weights, or infer
voice quality from the number of compliant cards. Report uncovered dimensions
and genuine unknowns explicitly.

## Pairwise contrast

For a comparative claim, select spans serving the same scene function and apply
the same question, evidence threshold, and exceptions to both. Show where the
reader experience differs and why the cited words support that reading. Do not
praise an imagined response in one candidate while dismissing an equivalent
practical thought in another without establishing a functional difference.
Check proxies such as ornate language, bodily detail, explicit emotion,
technical continuity, or similarity to a rule's wording against the actual
card. These features carry no generic bonus or penalty.

Return a supported preference, a tie, or an unresolved comparison. Distinguish
local evidence from holistic judgment. If naturalness or rhythm feels different
but its cause is uncertain, locate the impression and say the cause is unknown;
do not manufacture a deterministic explanation.

## Author feedback and evaluator validation

After unblinding, preserve author ratings and corrections literally. Report
ordering agreement, misses, ties/abstentions, and coverage with denominators
when measured. Do not treat author's numeric scores as explanations. Mark an
AI explanation as a hypothesis until the author supplies supporting feedback.
An evaluator disagreement can remain unresolved even where mechanism coverage
looks strong; preserve that result.

For explicitly authorized training, freeze a proposed change before testing.
Compare old/new contracts on identical surfaces and use separate future or
held-out controls. Record model/context changes as confounds. Known-score
retrospective analysis is diagnosis, not independent validation. Keep held-out
sources and expected outcomes away from candidate writers and evaluators until
their relevant judgments are frozen. Follow [style-calibration.md](style-calibration.md)
for author approval and promotion boundaries.

Deliver the annotations, supported contrasts, correctness findings on their own
axis, holistic judgment, score disagreements if available, and the smallest
bounded mechanism hypothesis. Do not silently rewrite prose, promote profiles,
start another calibration round, or claim that improved documentation repaired
style quality.
